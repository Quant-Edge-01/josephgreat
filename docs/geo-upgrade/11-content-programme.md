# Content programme: documenting the AI-discovery experiment

Working series title: “I'm trying to make ChatGPT recommend my Toronto marketing studio.”

The series establishes a dated record of what Joseph did and learned. It does not by itself prove expertise or successful AI referrals.

## Episodes and evidence gates

| Episode | Hook / useful point | What must exist before publishing |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Starting point | “I built the site. Now I'm testing whether people can actually find it through AI search.” | State Phase 1 already happened. Show the frozen prompt dataset. Say results are pending until run |
| 2. Baseline | “Here is what happened across 50 real small-business questions.” | Complete raw run and honest n/N; no fabricated “ChatGPT doesn't recommend us” premise |
| 3. OAI-SearchBot | “Letting a crawler in is one step. It doesn't make you the answer.” | Official OpenAI source; show real robots configuration |
| 4. What changed | “Our site explained the style. Some buying questions still needed clearer answers.” | Before/after passages and actual deployed revision |
| 5. A mistake | “We had to distinguish conversations, leads and bookings.” | Exact case-source correction; no invented failed campaign |
| 6. Competitor evidence | “These sources kept appearing in the test answers.” | Measured repeated competitors and saved citations, not a normal web-search sample |
| 7. 30-day update | “Here's what changed, and what I can't attribute to the site.” | Same-condition rerun; exact counts; date and confounders |
| 8. 60-day update | “Which pages got found, and did anyone contact us?” | Rerun plus actual referral/enquiry evidence; zero is acceptable |
| 9. Real referral | “This owner said they found us through ChatGPT.” | Real voluntary customer account, permission, and distinguish self-report from technical attribution |

## Production format

A 30–45 second reel: one claim, one screen/receipt, one practical explanation, one limitation and one next test. Keep the red-mask/surreal identity as the visual wrapper. Use captions readable on a phone. Never animate a fabricated ChatGPT response and present it as a recording.

Suggested cadence: one evidence-backed episode per week, with updates only when data exists. A missed result is not a reason to invent a new claim.

## First episode draft

“I run Joseph The Great, a small creative marketing studio in Toronto. I tested50 real small-business questions in fresh ChatGPT chats with search enabled. Joseph appeared in none of the saved answers. One answer asked for location, and another gave advice without naming marketers. The test ran in two periods, and I created a Google profile between them, so this isn't a clean before-and-after experiment. I'm keeping the records and testing clearer service pages, real evidence and outside client confirmation. I'll show what changes—including if nothing improves.”

Draft reflects the completed interrupted sample, not a deterministic ranking or proven failure of GEO. Review authentic excerpts and public disclosure before publication.

## Evidence and public archive

Keep the complete benchmark and raw answers in the private project. For a public update, publish an anonymised methods summary, selected authentic excerpts with source links, exact date/model/search conditions and the aggregate counts. Disclose that Joseph owns the studio and is running the experiment.

A methods article on Joseph's own site is first-party documentation. An outside editor's independent analysis is a different form of corroboration. Do not disguise a guest article as independent reporting.

## Commercial CTA

Use the existing free-three-ideas offer for owners who want to discuss their actual business. Do not sell a guaranteed “ChatGPT #1” service or imply the experiment has already produced clients.
