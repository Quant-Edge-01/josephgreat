## After the first completed run

1. Normalise competitor names and domains; preserve raw response wording.
2. Count responses mentioning each business, at most once per response. Divide by completed comparable prompts, not assumed 50.
3. Select the top five to ten repeated businesses by response count. Break ties using breadth across intent groups, not subjective preference.
4. Inspect the exact URLs ChatGPT cited first. A competitor with no source link gets “citation unavailable”, not an inferred citation.
5. Then inspect relevant service/pricing/case pages, independently hosted reviews, client-side sources, directories, geography and editorial reporting.
6. Record existence, actual supporting claim, date, ownership/independence and uncertainty for each source.
7. Write gaps as specific comparisons: “Competitor has a client-confirmed dated case with bookings; Joseph's case stops at conversations.” Only use this wording after both facts are verified.
8. Prioritise relevance × evidence strength × feasibility, not backlinks or domain counts alone.

## Evidence matrix for every repeated business

Columns: benchmark prompt IDs; mention count; cited URLs; source domain; service pages; exact price/currency/scope; case results/timeframe; independent review URL/count/date; directory; local presence proof; press; service specialisation; what Joseph has; missing equivalent; action; owner; status.

Use UNKNOWN rather than zero for uninspected review counts or missing snippets. An answer citing a domain does not expose the model's internal selection mechanism. Keep “stated evidence” separate from “analyst hypothesis”.
